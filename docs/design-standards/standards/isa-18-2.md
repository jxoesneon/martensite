# ANSI/ISA-18.2 — Management of Alarm Systems

**Config key:** `isa-18-2`

ANSI/ISA-18.2 is the process-industry standard for alarm-system
lifecycle management. It exists because alarm floods — dozens of
simultaneous alarms during an upset — were a documented contributor to
major industrial accidents: operators physically cannot triage alarms
faster than a bounded rate, and alarms beyond that rate are noise that
*masks* the one alarm that matters.

## Why it matters

The standard's key published numbers are rates and distributions:

- **~150 alarms/day** average is "very likely acceptable"; sustained
  peaks above **~10 alarms per 10 minutes** are unmanageable.
- Operators can effectively respond to roughly **1–2 alarms per 10
  minutes** when each requires analysis and action.
- Priority distributions should be skewed — a handful of critical
  alarms, not a democracy where everything screams.

A UI cannot cause a process alarm flood, but it has a design-time
analog: a screen where many elements simultaneously use alert-level
signals (alarm colors, badge storms, "critical" chrome) trains users
to ignore all of them. The lint rule applies ISA-18.2's logic to the
static scene: simultaneous alert-level signals above a small budget
means none of them are alerting anyone.

## Rules that enforce it

- [alert-saturation](../rules/alert-saturation.md) — simultaneous
  alert/alarm-colored elements per surface; the design-time alarm-flood
  analog. (Cites both `isa-18-2` and `wcag`, since an indistinguishable
  alert field is also a use-of-color failure.)
