# ANSI/ISA-101 — Human Machine Interfaces for Process Automation

**Config key:** `isa-101`

ANSI/ISA-101.01 is the process-industry standard for "High-Performance
HMI" — the discipline that grew out of post-accident analyses showing
that operators facing colorful, dense, animated control-room displays
detected abnormal situations *slower* than operators facing quiet gray
ones. The High-Performance HMI Handbook (Hollifield et al.) and the
ASM Consortium research behind it are among the most empirically
tested UI guidance that exists.

## Why it matters

ISA-101's core insight transfers to every dense information UI: **a
display's job is to make the abnormal obvious.** Two mechanisms do
that:

- **Color discipline.** Saturated color is reserved for abnormal
  states. Normal operation is rendered in muted gray-scale with
  analog information. If decoration spends the saturated-color budget,
  nothing stands out when something goes wrong — the channel is spent.
- **Progressive display hierarchy (L1–L4).** Level 1 is a whole-plant
  overview; Level 2 a unit; Level 3 equipment detail; Level 4
  diagnostics. Detail is *disclosed progressively*, never stacked.
  Widgets declare their level with the `@level:N` marker (N = 1–4).

## Rules that enforce it

- [nav-depth](../rules/nav-depth.md) — stacked orientation chrome is
  the desktop-UI analog of losing the L1–L4 thread.
- [chrome-ratio](../rules/chrome-ratio.md) — furniture consuming the
  surface that should carry process information.
- [color-budget](../rules/color-budget.md) — saturated hues in normal
  state spend the alarm channel.
- [color-only-info](../rules/color-only-info.md) — ISA-101 requires
  redundant coding (shape/label + color) for state.
- [progressive-disclosure](../rules/progressive-disclosure.md) —
  surfaces with many controls and no Expander/Sheet/Drawer/Popover/Menu
  affordance are flat L4-everywhere displays.

## External references

- ANSI/ISA-101.01-2015, *Human Machine Interfaces for Process
  Automation Systems* — the standard itself (ISA, paywalled).
- Hollifield, Oliver, Nimmo, Habibi — *The High-Performance HMI
  Handbook*, PAS, 2008.
- The ASM Consortium (*Abnormal Situation Management*) research
  program — the empirical basis for color discipline and display
  hierarchy.
- Endsley, M. R. (1995). "Toward a theory of situation awareness in
  dynamic systems." *Human Factors* 37(1) — the three-level
  situation-awareness model (perception → comprehension → projection)
  the hierarchy serves.
